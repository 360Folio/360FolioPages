03

Presence is a production problem, not a hardware one.

The headset is not what makes a place feel real. Framing, exposure and completeness are.

03.1

A flat photograph asks people to imagine.

Video is closer, but the viewer is still watching someone else's decisions about where to look. An interactive sphere removes both problems: the visitor turns towards the window, looks up at the ceiling, leans into the detail.

That is the difference between seeing a picture of a place and having been there.

03.2

The sphere has to be complete.

Real floor, real ceiling, nothing patched. No sign of the crew, the tripod, their shadows or their reflections anywhere in the frame — which is a capture discipline, not a retouching one.

It is also the fastest way to tell a serious tour from a cheap one: look down.

03.3

Exposure is where cheap tours fail.

Every frame is an HDR blend, so a bright window and a dark corner are both legible in the same view. It is what makes an interior look like the room you remember rather than a photograph of it.

We use HDR on all our work. It has never been an upgrade.
